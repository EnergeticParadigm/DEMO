# ERPL Demonstration — 03 | Research design and evidence

## Method

Use a structured comparison of reported research outputs. For each case distinguish the task assigned by humans, the AI-generated result, the nature of external evaluation, and the limits of the evidence.

## Findings

| Evidence | Sakana AI Scientist-v2 workshop experiment | Google AI co-scientist 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Research activity | AI-generated computational research and manuscripts | AI-generated biomedical hypotheses and proposals |
| Evaluation | Three selected manuscripts received workshop reviews; one received a mean score of 6.33/10 | Reported laboratory evaluation of selected hypotheses and computational recovery of a previously unpublished finding |
| Qualification | Manuscript exceeded the workshop's average acceptance threshold; withdrawn under the experiment protocol before final publication | Empirical support for selected hypotheses; human scientists participated in validation |
| What remains unresolved | Whether peer-review success alone establishes independent novel discovery | Whether the full laboratory research process is independently autonomous |

The Sakana result is particularly informative because the manuscript reporting a negative experimental finding received favorable reviews. The absence of a positive discovery does not make a negative result scientifically worthless. Nor does a favorable review establish every aspect of novelty, reproducibility or independence.

Google's bacterial gene-transfer example illustrates a different distinction: recovering a result that was not public can provide evidence of independent hypothesis generation, without establishing chronological priority over the human researchers who had already obtained it.

## Interim judgment

The observations are consistent with H2. H1 overstates what a manuscript review can establish by itself. However, neither case is a controlled test of overall research-system superiority.

**Next research action:** refine the conclusion so that useful negative results and independent rediscovery are not mistakenly excluded from scientific contributions.
